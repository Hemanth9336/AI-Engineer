Kestrel Bank's procurement team sends back your statement of work with a new clause: "The supplier guarantees that the assistant's answers are 100% accurate. Each inaccurate answer incurs a penalty of 500 EUR."

### Q1. Why can't you accept this clause?

- A. 500 EUR is too low
- B. Accuracy can't be measured
- C. Banks don't sign contracts
- D. No LLM system can guarantee 100% accuracy; you'd be promising something impossible and accepting unlimited liability

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: D** - Impossible promises become disputes.

</details>

### Q2. What do you propose instead?

- A. Remove all quality commitments
- B. Accuracy measured by the model itself
- C. 99.9% accuracy
- D. A measurable bar on an agreed golden set (for example 92% correct, 100% trap refusal), regular reporting, a fix-and-retest process for reported errors, and human review for high-risk answers

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: D** - Measurable commitments with a process behind them.

</details>

### Q3. Procurement asks what protects the bank if answers are wrong. What's the best answer?

- A. Nothing
- B. Insurance only
- C. Design safeguards: citations staff can check, refusals when unsure, human approval for anything binding, logging and audits - plus the eval gate before every release
- D. A disclaimer in small print

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: C** - Protection comes from the system design, not the penalty clause.

</details>
