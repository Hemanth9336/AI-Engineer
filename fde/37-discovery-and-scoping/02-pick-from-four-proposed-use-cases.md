Dockside Retail (220 stores) offers four candidate projects for an 8-week engagement:

1. A chatbot that answers store staff questions about HR policies (300 pages of PDFs; ~4,000 questions a month to HR today).
2. An AI that predicts next year's sales for every product.
3. An assistant that writes marketing copy for the website.
4. An agent that approves or rejects employee expense claims automatically.

### Q1. Which is the strongest first use case?

- A. 2 - forecasting is the most valuable
- B. 3 - marketing copy is easy
- C. 1 - high volume, answers already in documents, clear metrics (HR ticket volume, answer time) and low risk with citations and refusals
- D. 4 - approvals save the most time

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: C** - Volume + documents + measurable + low-risk.

</details>

### Q2. Why is option 2 a poor fit for this engagement?

- A. Forecasting is impossible
- B. It's a numerical forecasting problem better served by classical ML on sales data - not the LLM and RAG skills the engagement is about
- C. Stores don't sell products
- D. It's too cheap

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: B** - Match the problem to the right tool.

</details>

### Q3. What's the main concern with option 4?

- A. Fully automatic decisions about employees' money are high-risk: errors cost people money and may break labour rules - it needs human review at minimum
- B. It's too simple
- C. Expenses are rare
- D. It needs a bigger model

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: A** - Automated decisions on people need strong controls.

</details>
