At Harbor Foods' steering meeting, the CEO says: "Forget email drafts. I want an AI that reads our suppliers' contracts and automatically renegotiates prices when market prices change. That's the real value." The contracts are scanned PDFs in three languages; nobody has a list of which contracts allow renegotiation.

### Q1. What's the best response in the room?

- A. Agree - the CEO decides
- B. Say AI can't do contracts
- C. Acknowledge the goal (margin protection), explain the specific blockers - no reliable data, legal risk of automated negotiation, no metric yet - and propose a scoped first step
- D. Stay silent and raise it later

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: C** - Respect the goal, be specific about the risks, offer a path.

</details>

### Q2. What smaller step could serve the CEO's goal?

- A. Extract key terms (price clauses, renegotiation rights, renewal dates) from the contracts into a reviewed table, so procurement can see which contracts can be renegotiated
- B. Build the renegotiation agent anyway
- C. Translate all contracts
- D. Wait a year

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: A** - Extraction with review builds the data the big idea needs.

</details>

### Q3. How do you keep the original email-drafting project on track?

- A. Drop it
- B. Do both at once in 8 weeks
- C. Keep it as phase 1 with its agreed metrics, add the contract extraction as a candidate phase 2 with conditions, and record both in the scope document
- D. Let the CEO choose without the data

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: C** - Sequence, don't stack.

</details>
