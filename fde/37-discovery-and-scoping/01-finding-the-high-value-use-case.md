Customers arrive with a list of AI ideas. Your first job is to find the one that's **worth doing first** - valuable, feasible, measurable - and to make the rest wait.

## Where value hides

Look for work that is:

- **High volume and repetitive** - thousands of similar questions, documents or tickets a month.
- **Text-heavy** - answers live in documents, emails, manuals, policies.
- **Slow or expensive today** - queues, overtime, backlogs, outsourced teams.
- **Tolerant of a human in the loop** - drafts reviewed by staff before they go out.

## Questions that find it

| Ask | Listen for |
|---|---|
| "Walk me through a typical day for the team." | Repeated lookups, copy-paste, the same answer typed 50 times |
| "Where do the answers come from today?" | Documents (good for RAG) vs people's heads (hard) |
| "How do you know a good job was done?" | An existing metric you can move |
| "What happens when it goes wrong?" | The cost of a mistake - sets how much review you need |
| "Who would use it, and who would have to approve it?" | Users, owners, blockers |

## Score the candidates

| | Value | Feasibility | Measurable | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Draft replies for supplier desk | High: 2,300 emails/month | High: answers in docs | Reply time, edit rate | Low: human sends |
| Auto-resolve invoice disputes | High | Low: SAP writes, finance rules | Yes | High: money moves |
| Public chatbot | Medium | Medium | Harder | Medium: no human check |

The winner is rarely the most exciting idea. It's the one where **the data exists, the metric exists, and a mistake is cheap** - so you can prove value in weeks and earn the right to do the harder ones.
