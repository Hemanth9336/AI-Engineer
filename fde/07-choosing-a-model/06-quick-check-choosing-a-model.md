### Q1. What is the selection rule taught in this topic?

- A. Always the largest model
- B. The cheapest model that meets the quality and latency requirements
- C. The newest model
- D. The fastest model

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: B** - Requirements first, then cost.

</details>

### Q2. Which workload most clearly fits a small model?

- A. Reviewing merger contracts
- B. Classifying 40,000 tickets a day into six queues
- C. Multi-step financial planning
- D. Writing a legal opinion

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: B** - Easy, high-volume and easy to check.

</details>

### Q3. For live chat, which latency figure matters most to the user?

- A. Training time
- B. Time to first token
- C. Batch throughput
- D. Embedding speed

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: B** - Users watch the screen; the answer starting quickly matters most.

</details>

### Q4. A model just misses the quality bar. What's often the cheaper fix than a bigger model?

- A. Higher temperature
- B. Removing the system message
- C. Longer max_tokens
- D. A better prompt or better retrieval

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: D** - Many quality gaps close with better instructions or context.

</details>
