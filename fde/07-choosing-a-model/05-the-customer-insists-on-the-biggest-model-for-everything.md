Kestrel Bank's head of digital says: "We only want the best. Use the largest model for every feature - classification, chat, document review. Cost isn't a concern." The classification feature will run 2 million times a month.

### Q1. What is your first move?

- A. Show measured quality, latency and monthly cost per model for each feature, so the choice is based on numbers
- B. Agree - the customer is paying
- C. Refuse to use the largest model at all
- D. Pick the smallest model without telling them

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: A** - Make the trade-off visible with their own workload's numbers.

</details>

### Q2. On classification, the small model scores 96% and the largest 97%, at 25x the cost. What do you recommend?

- A. The largest - 1% matters
- B. Neither
- C. The small model for classification, and keep the large model where the quality gap is real
- D. A reasoning model

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: C** - A 1-point gain rarely justifies 25x the cost at 2M requests a month - and latency gets worse too.

</details>

### Q3. Which argument usually lands best with an executive who says cost isn't a concern?

- A. Models are all the same
- B. Latency: agents wait three times longer for every classified ticket, and the monthly bill appears in next year's budget review
- C. The vendor prefers small models
- D. Big models hallucinate more

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: B** - Tie the choice to user experience and the budget conversation that will happen later.

</details>
