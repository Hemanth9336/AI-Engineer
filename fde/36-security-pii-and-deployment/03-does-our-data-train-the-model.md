In the first security review at Kestrel Bank, the CISO asks: "If our staff paste customer complaints into this assistant, does that data end up training your model? Could another company's chatbot repeat our customers' details?"

### Q1. What's the honest, accurate first answer?

- A. 'No data is ever stored anywhere'
- B. 'Only a little'
- C. 'Models don't learn from data'
- D. 'Under the enterprise API terms we'd use, inputs and outputs aren't used for training - I'll get you the exact contract clauses, retention period and region so your team can verify it'

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: D** - Precise, verifiable, and backed by the actual terms.

</details>

### Q2. What should you avoid?

- A. Bringing documents
- B. Guessing or over-promising about retention, abuse monitoring or regions you haven't checked
- C. Mentioning redaction
- D. Involving the provider

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: B** - Security teams test claims; one wrong one costs trust.

</details>

### Q3. What extra safeguard can you offer regardless of the provider's terms?

- A. A bigger model
- B. Asking staff to be careful
- C. Turning off HTTPS
- D. Redacting PII before any text leaves the bank, and logging only redacted prompts with a short retention period

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: D** - Minimisation reduces risk whatever the contract says.

</details>
