Kestrel Bank's 8-week policy-assistant pilot ends next week. Agreed criteria: 90% correctness, 100% trap refusal, 60% weekly active staff. Results: correctness 84% (up from 71%), trap refusal 100%, weekly active staff 22%. The sponsor says: "Let's extend by three months - it's getting better."

### Q1. What do the numbers say?

- A. It's a success
- B. It's a total failure
- C. Nothing - pilots are subjective
- D. Safety is met, quality is improving but below target, and adoption is far below target - the pilot hasn't met its go criteria

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: D** - Read the numbers against the agreed criteria.

</details>

### Q2. What's the key question before any extension?

- A. Can we get a bigger budget?
- B. Who to blame
- C. Which model to use next
- D. Why staff don't use it - if adoption is blocked by something fixable (access, workflow, awareness), a short extension with a clear target may be justified; if they don't need it, more time won't help

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: D** - Low adoption is the critical failure; find its cause.

</details>

### Q3. If you extend, how should you do it?

- A. Short and specific: 4 weeks, new targets for adoption and correctness written down now, and a stop decision if they're missed
- B. Open-ended
- C. Change the criteria to match the results
- D. Add more use cases

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: A** - An extension without new criteria is how pilots become zombies.

</details>
