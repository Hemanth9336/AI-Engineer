Two weeks before the end of the Kestrel Bank engagement, you ask who will own the assistant. The answers: IT says "the business owns it", the business says "it's an IT system", and the person who maintained the golden set is moving to another department.

### Q1. Why is this a serious risk?

- A. It isn't - it'll keep running
- B. Without owners, nobody updates documents, reviews failures, runs evals or handles incidents - quality decays silently and the first outage has no responder
- C. The model will stop working
- D. It's only a paperwork issue

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: B** - Unowned AI systems drift and break quietly.

</details>

### Q2. What's the most effective step now?

- A. Write a longer runbook
- B. Escalate to the sponsor with a concrete ownership proposal - named people for system, golden set and on-call - and make it an exit criterion
- C. Extend your contract indefinitely
- D. Turn the assistant off

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: B** - Ownership needs a decision from someone with authority.

</details>

### Q3. How do you prove the new owners are ready?

- A. They say so
- B. They read the code
- C. They attend a presentation
- D. They run a prompt change through the evals and gate, handle a simulated incident from the runbook, and add a golden case - without your help

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: D** - Demonstrated, not declared.

</details>
