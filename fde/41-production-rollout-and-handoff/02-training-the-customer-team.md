The people who will own the assistant need different things from you. Train each group for the job they'll actually do.

| Group | They need to | Train with |
|---|---|---|
| **Engineers / IT** | Deploy, monitor, debug, re-index, change prompts safely | Pairing on real tasks; the runbook; a simulated incident |
| **Golden set owner** (often a senior user) | Add cases from feedback, review judge verdicts, own the bar | Working sessions on real failures |
| **End users** | Use it well, know its limits, give feedback | A 30-minute session; one-page guide; examples of good and bad use |
| **Business owner** | Read the weekly report, make decisions on scope and budget | A walkthrough of the report and the pilot criteria |

## Principles

- **Do, don't show.** People learn by running the evals themselves, not by watching you.
- **Use their incidents.** The best training material is the pilot's real failures - the skipped article, the timeout spike, the angry email.
- **Teach the why.** "We redact before sending because..." survives staff changes better than "run this script".
- **Write it down where they'll look.** The runbook in their repository, not a slide deck in your drive.

## Teach the limits too

End users must know what the assistant can't do: what's out of scope, why it says "I don't know", and that drafts need checking. Users who understand the limits trust the system more, not less.
