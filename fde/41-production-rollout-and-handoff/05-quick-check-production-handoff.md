### Q1. What's the strongest evidence the customer's team is ready?

- A. They attended training
- B. They've deployed a change, run the evals and handled a simulated incident without your help
- C. The runbook is long
- D. The sponsor signed off

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: B** - Demonstrated ability beats documents.

</details>

### Q2. When should handoff planning start?

- A. From the first weeks of the engagement, with exit criteria agreed early
- B. The last week
- C. After the pilot
- D. Never - stay on

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: A** - Ownership takes time to build.

</details>

### Q3. Who should own the golden set after handoff?

- A. A named domain expert on the customer's side, such as the desk team lead
- B. The model provider
- C. Nobody
- D. The FDE

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: A** - Correctness needs a domain owner.

</details>

### Q4. Why remove unnecessary components before leaving?

- A. To save disk
- B. Every component is something the customer must understand and maintain - simpler systems survive handoff
- C. Licensing
- D. Speed

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: B** - Simplicity is a handoff feature.

</details>
