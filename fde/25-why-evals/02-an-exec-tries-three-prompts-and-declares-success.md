At the end of a two-week sprint you demo Meridian's support assistant to their COO. She types three questions - all about tracking parcels - and gets three crisp, correct answers. "Brilliant. Let's roll it out to all 40 agents on Monday."

### Q1. What's the main problem with her conclusion?

- A. Three questions is too many
- B. Three hand-picked questions on one topic say nothing about the thousands of different questions agents will ask
- C. She should have used a bigger model
- D. The answers were too short

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: B** - A tiny, friendly sample is not evidence.

</details>

### Q2. What do you propose instead of 'no'?

- A. Ask for six months more
- B. Demo three more questions
- C. Roll out on Monday and fix bugs later
- D. Run the assistant on a golden set of ~100 real questions this week, share the score and failures, and roll out to five agents first if it passes the agreed bar

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: D** - Turn enthusiasm into a measured, staged rollout.

</details>

### Q3. She asks what 'passes the bar' means. Who should decide the bar?

- A. You, privately
- B. You and her together, before seeing the results - for example 90% correct and every out-of-scope question refused
- C. The model provider
- D. Whoever reads the results first

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer: B** - Agree the bar upfront, or the number will be argued after the fact.

</details>
