Almost every AI tool has a free tier and one or more paid plans. Knowing what the money buys helps you pick the right tool without overpaying - or leaking company data through a personal account.

## What the tiers usually look like

| Tier | Typical price | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| **Free** | $0 | A capable model with usage limits; sometimes a smaller model at busy times |
| **Individual paid** | About $20 a month | Higher limits, the strongest models, longer documents, extra features |
| **Power user** | $100-$200 a month | Very high limits for heavy daily use, often with the newest models first |
| **Team / Enterprise** | Per seat | Admin controls, single sign-on, and a promise not to train on your data |
| **API** | Pay per token | Direct access from code - the way this course works from topic 05 |

Prices change often, so check the provider's page before you buy. The shape - free, paid, team, API - has been stable.

## The data question

The biggest difference between a personal plan and a work plan is not the model, it is **what happens to your data**. On many consumer plans your conversations may be used to improve the product unless you switch that off. Business plans and the API normally do not train on your data and come with a contract that says so.

The rule of thumb: **never paste confidential company data into a personal AI account.** Use the tool your employer has approved. Topic 22 goes deeper on privacy.

## Choosing a tool

Ask four questions:

1. **Where does the work happen?** Writing in Docs points to the AI built into Docs. Code points to a coding assistant.
2. **Does it need fresh facts?** Use a tool with web search or AI search.
3. **How sensitive is the data?** Sensitive work belongs on an approved business plan or API.
4. **How much will you use it?** Light use is fine on a free tier; daily professional use justifies a paid one.

You do not need one perfect tool. Most people settle on one general chat assistant plus whatever AI is built into the apps they already use - and engineers add a coding assistant and the API. Next topic: what you can actually get done with them.
